**Title:** How Do Planes Stay Up in the Sky? #shorts

**Description:**

> A jumbo jet can weigh as much as 80 elephants, yet four forces keep it flying. Curved wings make air rush faster over the top, and the wing gets pushed up!
>
> WhyWhizz Asks #38. Facts checked against kid-friendly science sources.
>
> #shorts #kidsscience #learnwithkids #planes #howthingswork

**Tags:** kids science, science for kids, fun facts for kids, planes, howthingswork

**Sources checked:**

- NASA, 'What Is Aerodynamics? (Grades K-4)': the four forces of flight are lift, weight, thrust and drag; weight comes from gravity pulling down; wings are curved on top and flatter on the bottom, so air flows faster over the top and there is less air pressure on top

>
> WhyWhizz Asks #44. Facts checked against kid-friendly science sources.
>
> #shorts #kidsscience #learnwithkids #blackhole #space

**Tags:** kids science, science for kids, fun facts for kids, blackhole, space

**Sources checked:**

- NASA Knows, 'What Is a Black Hole? (Grades K-4)': a black hole is a place in space where gravity pulls so much that even light can not get out; the gravity is strong because matter has been squeezed into a tiny space, which can happen when a star is dying; black holes are invisible but scientists see how their gravity affects nearby stars and gas; even a black hole with the Sun's mass in the Sun's place would not make Earth fall in
- NASA Space Place, 'What Is a Black Hole?' and archived 'What are Black Holes Anyway?': nothing, not even light, can escape; supermassive black holes are thought to be at the centers of galaxies, including the Milky Way

- NASA Genesis mission fact sheet: the pull is only that strong close to a black hole, so black holes don't threaten us

**Description:**

>
> WhyWhizz Asks #45. Facts checked against kid-friendly science sources.
>
> #shorts #kidsscience #learnwithkids #earth #space

**Tags:** kids science, science for kids, fun facts for kids, earth, space

**Sources checked:**

- I'm a Scientist (Ireland): there isn't a scale big enough to weigh the Earth, so scientists calculate it; Earth includes crust, rock, mantle and liquid core
- I'm a Scientist (UK): scientists used gravity to find out Earth's mass, because the pull between two objects depends on both masses

- EarthDate (UT Austin): an average elephant weighs about 11,000 pounds (about 5 tonnes), so Earth weighs more than a sextillion elephants
